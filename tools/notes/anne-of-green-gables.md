# Anne of Green Gables — segment notes

What each segment covers and which threads are open, so a later segment's theme question can
reach back without rereading the book, and never forward. Theme evidence must quote this or an
earlier segment; the build rejects anything later.

Ages 10–12, sized at 150 words/min (~1500 words a segment). 65 segments.

**Segment starts reviewed:** all 65 (done up front). Moved or merged starts: ch1 (to the kitchen
door), ch4 (after breakfast), ch8 (Anne starts studying the prayer card), ch9–10 (the Marilla/Anne
scene after Mrs. Rachel leaves runs into ch10; next cut at the walk to Mrs. Lynde's), ch15 (into
school; "When school went out"), ch16 (the pantry), ch18 (the doctor at three o'clock), ch19 (next
morning's breakfast), ch25 ("the following fortnight"), ch26–28 (story club + Marilla's walk home;
"It was dark"; "unhappiness continued for a week" + Elaine setup; the flat pushed off), ch29
(sojourn in town), ch30–31 (Queen's class; Mrs. Lynde's visit + ch31 summer; Miss Stacy back),
ch32 (home from exams), ch33 (the drive to White Sands), ch34 ("lonesomer still"), ch37 (the
funeral, "Two days afterwards").

## Segments

1. **Ch 1, to the backyard.** Mrs. Rachel watches everything from her window; sees Matthew (shy,
   never goes anywhere) driving off in his best suit and white collar. Goes up to Green Gables
   after tea to find out. Green Gables sits far back from the road — "just staying", not living.
2. **Ch 1, in the kitchen.** Marilla, tall and thin, rigid, a hint of humor. They're getting a boy
   (~10–11) from an orphan asylum in Nova Scotia to help Matthew (sixty, "His heart troubles him");
   Mrs. Alexander Spencer is bringing him with her own girl. Rachel: "a mighty foolish thing—a risky
   thing"; horror stories. Marilla gave in because "It's so seldom Matthew sets his mind on
   anything". "I'd never dream of taking a girl." Rachel pities the orphan.
3. **Ch 2, the station.** Matthew dreads women. The stationmaster: a girl was dropped off; she stayed
   outside for "more scope for imagination". Described: ~11, ugly wincey dress, red braids,
   freckles. Would have slept in the wild cherry-tree. Matthew decides to take her home and let
   Marilla tell her. "I've never belonged to anybody."
4. **Ch 2, the drive.** Wild plum like a bride; wants a white dress someday; imagined a pale blue
   silk dress on the train to cheer up. Red roads, a thousand questions. Matthew "kind of liked her
   chatter"; "you can talk as much as you like". Big words for big ideas. Trees at the asylum
   "looked like orphans". Brook below the house. Red hair = "lifelong sorrow", can't be imagined
   away. Beautiful / clever / good. The Avenue.
5. **Ch 2, to Green Gables.** Silent in rapture; "White Way of Delight"; renaming places and
   people. "never had a real home". Barry's pond → "Lake of Shining Waters"; Diana Barry, about
   eleven, at Orchard Slope. Says good night to things she loves. Picks out Green Gables herself:
   "I felt it was home." Matthew dreads her disappointment.
6. **Ch 3.** Marilla: "Where is the boy?" "You don't want me because I'm not a boy!" Cordelia; Anne
   spelled with an E. Mrs. Spencer was told they wanted a girl. A boy "to help Matthew on the farm".
   "Depths of despair" at supper. The bare east gable; "the very worst night I've ever had". Matthew
   wants to keep her: "We might be some good to her." Marilla: "bewitched". Anne cries herself to
   sleep.
7. **Ch 4, morning.** Cherry-tree in bloom at the window; orchards, brook, the sea. "The worst of
   imagining things is that the time comes when you have to stop and that hurts." Forgot the
   bedclothes. Told to hold her tongue, silent and far away at breakfast. Matthew's silent
   persistence.
8. **Ch 4, after breakfast.** Washes dishes; Matthew a "kindred spirit". Won't go outside — would
   love it too much to leave. Names the geranium Bonny, the cherry-tree Snow Queen ("It makes them
   seem more like people"). Marilla: "She'll be casting a spell over me, too." Off to White Sands to
   see Mrs. Spencer; Matthew has hired Jerry Buote for the summer.
9. **Ch 5.** "you can nearly always enjoy things if you make up your mind firmly that you will."
   Redheads can't wear pink. "Perfect graveyard of buried hopes." History: born in Bolingbroke,
   Nova Scotia, eleven last March; parents Walter and Bertha Shirley, teachers, both died of fever
   when she was three months old. Mrs. Thomas (drunken husband), looked after her children; Mrs.
   Hammond (twins three times), up river; the asylum four months. Little school; loves poetry. They
   "meant to be" good to her. Marilla's pity stirs. The sea, gulls; "the end of everything".
10. **Ch 6.** Mrs. Spencer: Robert sent word by his flighty daughter Nancy, who said a girl. Mrs. Peter
    Blewett (shrewish, "terrible worker and driver") will take Anne. Anne's misery; Marilla won't hand
    her over; will talk to Matthew. Anne: Mrs. Blewett "looks exactly like a—like a gimlet." Matthew:
    "wouldn't give a dog I liked to that Blewett woman." Marilla: "she may stay"; Matthew isn't to
    interfere with her methods; "one of the sort you can do anything with if you only get her to love
    you." Anne not told till tomorrow.
11. **Ch 7.** Fold your clothes. "I never say any prayers." Knows the catechism (likes the grand
    words). Would pray alone in a field looking at the sky. Prayer: thanks for the White Way, the
    Lake, Bonny, the Snow Queen; asks to stay and be good-looking; "Yours respectfully". Marilla will
    teach her properly, Sunday-school.
12. **Ch 8, first part.** Marilla finds her smart, obedient, a daydreamer. Tells her they'll keep
    her; Anne cries, happy. School in September. "Just plain Marilla," not Aunt Marilla ("as if I
    really belonged to you"). Marilla doesn't imagine things. The picture "Christ Blessing Little
    Children": Anne is the lonely girl who "didn't belong to anybody".
13. **Ch 8, second part.** Learns the Lord's Prayer. Wants a "bosom friend", a kindred spirit;
    Diana Barry (black hair, pretty), Mrs. Barry "very particular". Katie Maurice (reflection in
    Mrs. Thomas's bookcase door) and Violetta (echo). Wants to be a bee. Imagines a grand room, Lady
    Cordelia Fitzgerald — can't; "a million times nicer to be Anne of Green Gables than Anne of
    nowhere in particular." Good afternoon to Snow Queen and the gray house (Orchard Slope).
14. **Ch 9, to Mrs. Rachel's exit.** A fortnight later; Rachel had grippe. Anne has explored every
    tree, the spring, log bridge, woods. Marilla: "The house seems a different place already."
    Rachel: "skinny and homely", freckles, "hair as red as carrots". Anne: "I hate you", "fat and
    clumsy", "I'll never forgive you". Marilla: "You shouldn't have twitted her about her looks";
    "she's never been taught what is right". Rachel: use a birch switch; "Her temper matches her
    hair"; offended exit.
15. **Ch 9 end – ch 10, Anne agrees.** Marilla can't whip a child. "such a difference between saying
    a thing yourself and hearing other people say it". Marilla's memory: an aunt called her "a dark,
    homely little thing". Stay in her room till she'll apologize. Matthew: "a meddlesome old gossip";
    don't be too hard. Matthew sneaks upstairs (first time in four years): "do it and have it over";
    Anne is sorry now, will do it for him; secret kept.
16. **Ch 10, the apology.** Anne rapt and radiant on the way; kneels, dramatic apology, enjoying it.
    Rachel forgives: "I kind of like her"; red hair may turn "a real handsome auburn"; a quick temper
    isn't sly. "Handsome is as handsome does." "a lovely, comfortable feeling to apologize and be
    forgiven". Hand in Marilla's: "It's lovely to be going home and know it's home"; Marilla's warm
    throb "of the maternity she had missed".
17. **Ch 11.** Three plain dresses; Anne wanted puffed sleeves ("I'd rather look ridiculous when
    everybody else does than plain and sensible all by myself"). Sunday-school alone (Mrs. Lynde gone);
    wreath of buttercups and wild roses on her hat. Girls whisper (Jerry Buote says she talks to the
    trees). Miss Rogerson not a kindred spirit; every girl had puffed sleeves. Mr. Bell's long prayer,
    the sermon lacks imagination — Marilla secretly agrees.
18. **Ch 12.** Scolded for the flower hat (Rachel told). Offers to go back to the asylum; Marilla: no.
    Meets Diana at Orchard Slope; Mrs. Barry strict, Diana reads too much. Vow in the garden "as long
    as the sun and moon shall endure." Playhouse plans; Dryad's Bubble (the spring). Matthew's chocolate
    sweeties — half for Diana. Marilla: "only three weeks... it seems as if she'd been here always";
    "I'm getting fond of her".
19. **Ch 13.** August. Late from Idlewild (the playhouse in a birch ring), Matthew a "sympathetic
    listener". Sunday-school picnic next week, ice cream; Marilla will bake a basket; Anne kisses her
    (first time a child had). Cooking lessons promised. Patchwork has no scope. Fairy glass, Willowmere.
    "looking forward to things is half the pleasure of them". Marilla's amethyst brooch (mother's, a
    braid of her hair) — most treasured possession; Anne admires it.
20. **Ch 14, first part.** Brooch missing. Anne tried it on but says she put it back. Marilla doesn't
    believe her — lying worse than temper, "Slyness and untruthfulness". Matthew: behind the bureau?
    Room until she confesses; no picnic otherwise.
21. **Ch 14, second part.** Anne's invented confession (dropped it off the bridge into the Lake of
    Shining Waters) — no picnic anyway; despair; "You'll feel remorse of conscience someday".
    Marilla finds the brooch caught in her black lace shawl. Anne confessed to get to the picnic.
    Marilla: "I was wrong... I shouldn't have doubted your word"; forgive each other. Picnic:
    "scrumptious", ice cream "sublime". Marilla: "I've learned a lesson"; "no house will ever be dull
    that she's in."
22. **Ch 15, the way to school.** Lover's Lane, Willowmere, Violet Vale, the Birch Path (Diana named
    it). Mr. Phillips (making eyes at Prissy Andrews, 16, studying for Queen's). Anne behind (fourth
    book). First compliment: a pretty nose. Pooled lunches. Gilbert Blythe back from New Brunswick,
    "aw'fly handsome", teases girls; Charlie Sloane likes Anne. Gilbert nearly fourteen, was in Alberta
    with his sick father. Josie Pye peeped in spelling; Pye girls cheats.
23. **Ch 15, the slate.** Gilbert pins Ruby Gillis's braid, winks. "Carrots! Carrots!" — Anne cracks
    her slate over his head. Gilbert: "It was my fault." Stands at the blackboard: "Ann Shirley has a
    very bad temper" (no e). Won't forgive him; crow vs carrots. Next day: late from the spruce grove
    (rice-lily wreath), made to sit with Gilbert. Grinds his candy heart ("You are sweet") under her
    heel.
24. **Ch 15, quitting school.** Takes her things home; won't go back to Mr. Phillips. Rachel: humor
    her, Mr. Phillips was in the wrong, the scholars took Anne's side. Anne learns at home; ices
    Gilbert. Cries over Diana's imagined wedding; Marilla laughs so hard Matthew wonders when he heard
    that before.
25. **Ch 16, first part.** October ("so glad I live in a world where there are Octobers"). Marilla
    to the Aid at Carmody; Anne may have Diana to tea. Not the rosebud spray tea set (minister/Aids
    only). Raspberry cordial "on the second shelf of the sitting-room closet". Prim, grown-up greeting;
    the orchard; school gossip (Gertie Pye, Ruby's warts, everybody misses Anne); Anne won't hear about
    Gilbert.
26. **Ch 16, the "cordial".** Bottle found on the top shelf; Diana drinks three big tumblers ("doesn't
    taste a bit like" Mrs. Lynde's). Anne's stories: forgot the flour (daydreaming of nursing Diana
    through smallpox), the mouse in the uncovered pudding sauce before Mrs. Chester Ross. Diana dizzy,
    goes home before tea.
27. **Ch 16, fallout.** Mrs. Barry: Anne set Diana drunk; never to play again. Marilla finds it was her
    currant wine; the cordial was in the cellar. Marilla fails with Mrs. Barry ("unreasonable"); Anne
    goes herself — her big words make Mrs. Barry think she's mocking (unlike Mrs. Lynde). "not a fit
    little girl". Marilla laughs with Matthew, then kisses sleeping Anne: "Poor little soul."
28. **Ch 17.** Farewell at the Dryad's Bubble, ten minutes: Diana "I love you devotedly" — Anne never
    thought anybody could love her. Lock of Diana's hair. "little dream girls are not satisfying after
    a real friend." Anne goes back to school; welcomed with gifts; ignores Gilbert's strawberry apple,
    accepts Charlie Sloane's pencil. Notes with Diana (sitting with Gertie Pye; Anne with Minnie
    Andrews). Rivalry with Gilbert (good-natured on his side; Anne holds grudges); both promoted to the
    fifth class; geometry is her Waterloo.
29. **Ch 18, first part.** January: the Premier in Charlottetown; Marilla goes with Mrs. Rachel. Anne
    and Matthew at home; Matthew heard she's "the smartest scholar". Anne learned what makes the roads
    red. Conservative like Matthew. Locks Jane's book in the jam closet. Diana: Minnie May (three) has
    croup, parents away. Matthew goes for the doctor. Anne knows croup from the Hammond twins; ipecac;
    works all night with Diana and Young Mary Joe.
30. **Ch 18, second part.** Doctor at three (had to go to Spencervale): "she saved that baby's life".
    Anne glad of the Hammond twins after all. Mrs. Barry sorry, friends again; best china, "as if I
    was real company". Anne "a soul above red hair". When grown up she'll talk to little girls as if
    they were too and never laugh at big words.
31. **Ch 19, first part.** Candle-and-cardboard signals. Diana's birthday: concert with the Murray
    cousins, spare-room bed. Marilla says no; Matthew keeps saying "I think you ought to let Anne go";
    she gives in. "Matthew understands me, and it's so nice to be understood." Gilbert beats Anne in
    class that day.
32. **Ch 19, the concert.** Dressing up; plain coat vs Diana's fur cap — uses her imagination. Pung
    sleigh. Thrills; Mr. Phillips looks at Prissy; Anne reads a book through Gilbert's recitation
    (Diana: he looked right at you). Race into the spare-room bed — onto Aunt Josephine (Diana's
    father's aunt, ~70, Charlottetown, prim).
33. **Ch 19, Miss Barry.** She's furious, leaving early, won't pay for Diana's music lessons. Mrs.
    Lynde: "too heedless and impulsive"; "Look before you leap". Diana no telltale. Anne confesses:
    her idea; be cross with me, not Diana; "Have you any imagination, Miss Barry?"; "a little orphan
    girl who had never had such an honor". Miss Barry laughs, stays to know "that Anne-girl", silver
    bangle for Diana; invites Anne to her "very sparest spare-room bed" in town. "Kindred spirits are
    not so scarce as I used to think."
34. **Ch 20.** Spring; Mayflowers ("souls of the flowers that died last summer"); daring is
    fashionable; Anne scorns Gilbert's Mayflowers. "such a lot of different Annes in me". June: one
    year at Green Gables ("the turning point in my life"); Marilla "not exactly sorry". Burned pie
    and starched handkerchiefs (enchanted-princess daydream; Victoria Island). The Haunted Wood — Anne
    and Diana invented it (white lady, headless man); Marilla makes her walk through it at dusk.
    "I'll be contented with commonplace places after this."
35. **Ch 21, first part.** Mr. Phillips leaves; everyone cries ("because all the others did"). New
    minister Mr. Allan and wife (puffed sleeves), boarding at Mrs. Lynde's. Old Mr. Bentley; the
    trial preachers (Mr. Terry's imagination ran away "as I did mine in the matter of the Haunted
    Wood"). Mrs. Allan a kindred spirit; lets the class ask questions; religion cheerful. Tea planned
    for Wednesday; Anne may make a layer cake; keep it from shy Matthew.
36. **Ch 21, the tea.** Preparations; dryad (Diana's mother angry about the Haunted Wood). Cold in
    the head. Cake rises; Anne decorates the table; coaxes Matthew to attend. Cake flavored with
    Anodyne Liniment (Marilla had poured it into a vanilla bottle); Anne couldn't smell it for her
    cold. Mrs. Allan comforts her: "a funny mistake that anybody might make." "tomorrow is a new day
    with no mistakes in it yet"; never the same mistake twice; a limit to mistakes.
37. **Ch 22.** Letter to "Miss Anne Shirley" — tea at the manse. Marilla wants her calm; Anne all
    "spirit and fire and dew"; Marilla secretly likes her as she is. Rain fears. Marilla: think of
    Mrs. Allan, not yourself. Tea: Laurette Bradley; Anne to sing in the Sunday-school choir; White
    Sands Hotel concerts for the Charlottetown hospital; naturally good vs trying hard; Mrs. Allan a
    dunce at geometry too. New teacher: Miss Muriel Stacy.
38. **Ch 23, the ridgepole.** Small mistakes. Diana's party; dares; Josie Pye walks the fence; Anne
    brags of a Marysville girl; Josie dares her to walk Mr. Barry's kitchen ridgepole; "My honor is
    at stake." Falls; Mr. Barry carries her home. Marilla's revelation: Anne "dearer to her than
    anything else on earth." Faints; ankle broken.
39. **Ch 23, laid up.** "the thought that it is all my own fault is what makes it so hard". Couldn't
    bear Josie Pye's scorn. Seven weeks; imagination helps; visitors (Mr. Bell, Mrs. Allan fourteen
    times, Josie). "You find out how many friends you have." Miss Stacy: recitations every other
    Friday, field days, physical culture.
40. **Ch 24.** October, back to school. Miss Stacy brings out the best; Anne's compositions;
    geometry still hard. Wants to be "remarkable" — nurse or missionary. Christmas-night concert for a
    schoolhouse flag; Marilla: "rank foolishness". Anne in dialogues, recitations, the Hope tableau;
    Josie sulky; Ruby's slippers. Matthew free to "spoil Anne"; "a little 'appreciation' sometimes
    does quite as much good as all the conscientious 'bringing up' in the world."
41. **Ch 25, first part.** December; Matthew sees Anne "was not dressed like the other girls". A dress
    for Christmas. Lawson's store, Miss Lucilla Harris's bangles: a garden rake, hayseed, twenty pounds
    of brown sugar. Asks Mrs. Lynde, who'll choose brown gloria and make it, with puffs. Rachel: "flesh
    and blood don't come under the head of arithmetic"; Matthew "waking up after being asleep for over
    sixty years."
42. **Ch 25, Christmas.** Marilla grumbles about the sleeves ("as big as balloons"). White Christmas;
    the brown gloria dress with puffed sleeves — Anne's tears. Aunt Josephine sends kid slippers. The
    concert: Anne the star; thought of her puffed sleeves for courage; Diana saw Gilbert pocket Anne's
    fallen rose. Marilla proud "although I'm not going to tell her so"; Matthew told her. Queen's
    someday.
43. **Ch 26, first part.** Flat after the concert; feuds. Anne turns thirteen (Diana a month older).
    Trying to be like Mrs. Allan; "besetting sin is imagining too much and forgetting my duties". Nose
    vanity. "The Jealous Rival; or In Death Not Divided" (Cordelia, Geraldine, Bertram — ends in a
    funeral). Story club proposed.
44. **Ch 26 end – ch 27 start.** Story club (Jane, Ruby too): noms-de-plume (Rosamond Montmorency);
    morals; Aunt Josephine finds the tragic stories "amusing". Mrs. Allan was a mischief as a girl —
    encouraging. April: Marilla walks home glad to have Anne; nearly snapped at Rachel at the Aid for
    criticizing Anne; the fire is out, Anne gone. Matthew: don't judge too hasty.
45. **Ch 27, green hair.** Anne hiding in bed — hair dyed green (peddler's dye, promised raven black;
    fifty cents of chicken money). "worth while to be a little wicked to get rid of red hair." Believed
    him: "I know what it feels like to have your word doubted." Won't wash out. "tangled web".
46. **Ch 27 end – ch 28 start.** A week; Diana keeps the secret. Hair cut off; penance of the mirror;
    "I was vain". Josie: "a perfect scarecrow" — Anne says nothing, forgives her. Marilla's headaches
    worse. Elaine: Diana says Anne's hair is darker now, maybe auburn. Idlewild cut down by Mr. Bell;
    rowing on the pond. Anne plays Elaine in the flat.
47. **Ch 28, the lily maid.** Flat leaks (stake), Anne climbs a bridge pile; girls think she drowned.
    Gilbert rows by, rescues her, asks to be friends ("your hair is awfully pretty now"); Anne
    hesitates (heart gives "a quick, queer little beat") but says never; he'll never ask again. Odd
    regret. Mistakes list: brooch → meddling, Haunted Wood → imagination, liniment → carelessness, dye
    → vanity, now → too romantic. Matthew: "keep a little of it, Anne."
48. **Ch 29, first part.** September. Aunt Josephine invites Anne and Diana for the Exhibition.
    Marilla wouldn't let her go to the White Sands Hotel concert. New navy dress (Marilla makes them
    fashionable now); Matthew insisted on a new coat; blue velvet cap. Beechwood; Miss Barry: taller,
    better-looking. Velvet carpet and silk curtains — "no scope for imagination".
49. **Ch 29, town.** Exhibition: glad at Josie's prize (improving), Mrs. Lynde's butter; refuses to
    bet (would tell Mrs. Allan); fortune; spare room "isn't what I used to think it was"; Madame
    Selitsky; ice cream at eleven; "I wasn't born for city life". Miss Barry: a child like Anne would
    make her "a better and happier woman". Home: Marilla's broiled chicken, "fearful lonesome";
    "the best of it all was the coming home."
50. **Ch 30, first part.** November. Marilla's eyes tired (glasses). Her deep, undemonstrative love
    — stricter because Anne is so dear; Anne doesn't know. Anne almost fourteen; Miss Stacy on
    forming character by twenty. Ben Hur behind the history book (deceit) — forgiven. Miss Stacy's
    Queen's class offered; "a girl being fitted to earn her own living"; Anne wants to be a teacher; "a
    purpose in life".
51. **Ch 30, the Queen's class.** Gilbert, Anne, Ruby, Jane, Josie, Charlie, Moody Spurgeon; not Diana
    (Anne's tears). Plans (Jane never marry, Ruby marry, Josie "orphans who are living on charity").
    Open rivalry; Gilbert ignores Anne; she's forgiven him and would answer differently — too late;
    hides it. Winter, spring; Miss Stacy will come back. Anne locks away her books for summer; "the
    last summer I'll be a little girl".
52. **Ch 30 end – ch 31 start.** Matthew "a bad spell with his heart"; must avoid excitement and heavy
    work. Mrs. Rachel: Anne "a real smart girl", "I did make a mistake in judging Anne"; Anne pale and
    big-eyed, makes others look "common and overdone". Summer outdoors on the doctor's orders. Why
    can't women be ministers? Mrs. Lynde's nagging makes Anne (and Marilla) want to misbehave.
    "It's a serious thing to grow up"; only one chance. Grown two inches; green dress with a flounce.
53. **Ch 31, the last winter.** Entrance dread (bad dreams of Gilbert's name at the top). Miss Stacy's
    guidance; outings. Anne (fifteen) taller than Marilla; Marilla cries — she'll be away next winter;
    Matthew: branch railroad. Anne talks less, fewer big words ("keep them in one's heart, like
    treasures"; short words stronger). Story club gone; Miss Stacy's own-life stories; "my own severest
    critic". Stumbling blocks: geometry (Anne), Latin, algebra, arithmetic.
54. **Ch 32, exams.** Miss Stacy leaves Avonlea school; Diana stays behind. Number thirteen. Promise
    not to cram. At Beechwood. Letter: Moody Spurgeon reciting the multiplication table; felt as when
    she asked to stay at Green Gables; history hard; geometry tomorrow; cheered Moody.
55. **Ch 32, pass list.** Home Friday; geometry doubts; "I'd rather not pass at all than not come out
    pretty well up" (Gilbert). Wishes she'd made friends with him. "Nobler motive": Matthew's pride.
    Three weeks' wait; Matthew considers voting Grit. Diana runs with the paper: first, tied with
    Gilbert, name first, of two hundred; all passed. Matthew: "I knew you could beat them all easy";
    Mrs. Rachel: "a credit to your friends".
56. **Ch 33, dressing.** White Sands Hotel concert for the Charlottetown hospital; Anne to recite.
    East gable now dainty (matting, green muslin, Miss Stacy's photo) — the velvet carpet never came
    but "her dreams had kept pace with her growth". Diana's taste: white organdy "as if it grew on
    you"; pearl beads from Matthew; Madonna hair. Dimple-dream won't come true, but many have. Marilla
    grumbles, secretly proud. Anne to go to town next month.
57. **Ch 33, the concert.** Billy Andrews drives. Dressing room: feels countrified; white-lace girl
    sneers at "country bumpkins". Professional elocutionist Mrs. Evans. Stage fright — sees Gilbert
    smiling (she thinks taunting), won't fail before him; recites splendidly; encore "or Matthew will
    be disappointed". Pink lady (millionaire's wife). Artist: "splendid Titian hair... a face I should
    like to paint." Jane wants to be rich; Anne: "We are rich" (sixteen years, imaginations); "I don't
    want to be anyone but myself... content to be Anne of Green Gables, with my string of pearl beads."
58. **Ch 34, to Queen's.** Marilla's gift of green material for an evening dress. Anne recites; Marilla
    cries for "the little girl you used to be". Anne: "I'm only just pruned down and branched out. The
    real me—back here—is just the same." Matthew: "there never was a luckier mistake... Providence."
    Parting; Marilla weeps that night. Queen's: Anne and Gilbert take Second Year work (First Class
    license in one year); rivalry welcome ("What a splendid chin he has!"). Lonesome.
59. **Ch 34, first night.** Boarding-house (found by Miss Barry); homesick for her white room. Josie
    Pye (tactless; told Frank Stockley Anne was an adopted orphan); Jane and Ruby. Avery scholarship
    (highest mark in English; $250 a year for four years at Redmond) — Anne resolves to win it for
    Matthew; ambitions never end.
60. **Ch 35.** Friday walks home; Gilbert walks with Ruby Gillis; Anne would like him as a friend (no
    "silly sentiment"). Friends Stella Maynard and Priscilla Grant. Medal: Gilbert, Anne or Lewis
    Wilson. Rivalry without bitterness. Miss Barry: "she makes me love her". Spring exams; "the joy of
    the strife. Next to trying and winning, the best thing is trying and failing."
61. **Ch 36.** Gilbert medalist; Anne wins the Avery. Commencement: "Reckon you're glad we kept her,
    Marilla?" Home; Diana (Stella "dearest girl in the world except one"). Redmond in September. Gilbert
    must teach (father can't afford college). Matthew grayer, heart spells; Marilla's eye pain, oculist
    coming; the Abbey Bank shaky (all their savings). Evening with Matthew: "I'd rather have you than a
    dozen boys... my girl that I'm proud of." "the last night before sorrow touched her life".
62. **Ch 37, Matthew's death.** He collapses holding the paper (Abbey Bank failed) — sudden shock.
    Neighbors come. Anne tearless, wants to be alone; wakes in the night remembering "My girl—my girl
    that I'm proud of" and weeps; Marilla comforts her: "We've got each other"; "I love you as dear as
    if you were my own flesh and blood".
63. **Ch 37, after.** Funeral; life goes on; Anne feels disloyal for laughing — Mrs. Allan: Matthew
    liked to hear you laugh. Planted a slip of his mother's white Scotch rosebush on his grave. Marilla
    to see the eye specialist. Hair "auburn now" (except to Josie). Gilbert teaching; Marilla and John
    Blythe — a quarrel she wouldn't forgive; "I've always kind of wished I'd forgiven him".
64. **Ch 38, first part.** Oculist: stop reading/sewing/crying or be blind in six months. Mr. Sadler
    wants to buy Green Gables; Marilla must sell (money gone, notes to pay). Anne gives up Redmond and
    the Avery to stay and teach (Carmody, since Avonlea is promised to Gilbert); study at home; "no
    sacrifice"; "a bend in it... I'm going to believe that the best does." "You blessed girl!"
65. **Ch 38, end.** Avonlea talks; Mrs. Allan and Mrs. Lynde approve. Gilbert gave up the Avonlea
