# Alice's Adventures in Wonderland — segment notes

What each segment covers and which threads are open, so a later segment's theme question can
reach back without rereading the book, and never forward. Theme evidence must quote this or an
earlier segment; the build rejects anything later.

Edition: Gutenberg #28885, Arthur Rackham's 1907 pictures (12 colour plates, 10 line drawings kept).
Sized for age 8 (110 words/min, ~1,100 words a segment). Two segments per chapter; every even
segment carries the theme + written pair.

**Segment starts reviewed through:** all 24.

## Segments

1. **Ch 1, the fall.** Bored on the bank (a book with no pictures or conversations). White Rabbit
   with a watch and waistcoat-pocket; Alice follows it down the rabbit-hole "never once
   considering how in the world she was to get out again". Slow fall: cupboards, the empty ORANGE
   MARMALADE jar put back, Dinah the cat ("do cats eat bats?"). Lands on sticks and leaves; the
   Rabbit vanishes round a corner; long low hall.
2. **Ch 1, the hall.** Locked doors; glass table, tiny golden key; a 15-inch door behind a curtain
   to "the loveliest garden". DRINK ME (checks it isn't marked poison) → ten inches high; forgot
   the key on the table, can't reach it, cries, scolds herself ("fond of pretending to be two
   people"). EAT ME cake. Thread: "very few things indeed were really impossible"; getting used to
   the out-of-the-way. *Theme: expecting strange things.*
3. **Ch 2, first part.** Grows over nine feet ("Curiouser and curiouser!"), cries a pool. The
   Rabbit, dressed up for the Duchess, drops gloves and fan. "Who in the world am I?" — Ada,
   Mabel; multiplication and geography go wrong; "How doth the little crocodile" comes out
   different. The fan shrinks her; door shut again, key back on the table.
4. **Ch 2, the pool.** Slips into her own pool of tears. The Mouse: French "Où est ma chatte?";
   Alice keeps mentioning Dinah and a rat-killing terrier; apologises each time. Mouse promises
   its history (why it hates cats and dogs). Duck, Dodo, Lory, Eaglet; all swim ashore. *Theme:
   means well but talks before thinking.*
5. **Ch 3, the Caucus-race.** Wet party; Lory "older than you, and must know better". The Mouse's
   "driest thing" (William the Conqueror history). Dodo's Caucus-race: run when you like, "Everybody
   has won, and all must have prizes"; Alice hands out comfits and is given her own thimble.
6. **Ch 3, the tale.** Comfits eaten. "A long and sad tale" / tail (the tail-shaped poem). Mouse
   offended ("not attending", "a knot") and leaves. Old Crab: never lose your temper. Alice says
   Dinah eats birds; everyone makes excuses and goes. Alone, crying, misses Dinah; hears
   footsteps. *Theme: lonely, misses home.*
7. **Ch 4, the Rabbit's house.** Rabbit hunts the gloves and fan, takes Alice for Mary Ann,
   sends her home; she's too frightened to explain and obeys. W. RABBIT house; unlabelled bottle
   ("something interesting is sure to happen … whenever I eat or drink anything") → fills the
   room, arm out the window, foot up the chimney. "There ought to be a book written about me."
   Door opens inwards, blocked by her elbow; she snatches at the Rabbit (cucumber-frame); Pat.
8. **Ch 4, Bill and the puppy.** Ladders; Bill sent down the chimney, kicked out "like a
   sky-rocket". "Burn the house down!" — Alice threatens them with Dinah. Pebbles turn to cakes;
   she reasons a cake must make her smaller; escapes to a wood. Plan: right size, then the garden.
   Enormous puppy and the stick. Mushroom; Caterpillar with a hookah on top. *Theme: eating and
   drinking change her size.*
9. **Ch 5, the Caterpillar.** "Who are you?" — Alice doesn't know; "so many different sizes in a
   day is very confusing". "Keep your temper." Recites "You are old, Father William" wrong.
   Three inches is "wretched" (the Caterpillar is exactly three inches). "One side will make you
   grow taller, and the other side … shorter."
10. **Ch 5, the Pigeon.** Mushroom pieces: chin hits foot, then a neck like a serpent above the
    trees. Pigeon guarding its eggs calls her a serpent; "I'm a little girl," said "rather
    doubtfully". Nibbles back to normal; little house; shrinks to nine inches. *Theme: can't say
    who she is because she keeps changing.*
11. **Ch 6, the Duchess's kitchen.** Fish-Footman brings the Queen's croquet invitation;
    Frog-Footman's nonsense; a plate flies out. Kitchen full of pepper; Duchess, sneezing baby,
    cook, grinning Cheshire Cat. Cook throws pans; "chop off her head"; the rough lullaby. Duchess
    flings Alice the baby and leaves for the Queen's croquet.
12. **Ch 6, pig and Cat.** Alice carries the baby out so they won't kill it; it turns into a pig
    and trots off. Cheshire Cat: which way to go; Hatter and March Hare, "they're both mad"; "we're
    all mad here"; "You'll see me there" (croquet — open thread). Vanishes, leaving the grin. March
    Hare's house. *Theme: she cares about keeping others safe.*
13. **Ch 7, tea-party.** "No room!"; wine that isn't there; "Your hair wants cutting"; raven and
    writing-desk (no answer). Watch with butter in it, tells the day. Time: quarrelled with the
    Hatter at the Queen's concert ("He's murdering the time! Off with his head!"), so it's always
    six o'clock, always tea-time; they move round the table. Thread: the Hatter and the Queen.
14. **Ch 7, the Dormouse's story and the garden.** Three sisters in a treacle-well; Alice keeps
    interrupting; move one place on. "Then you shouldn't talk" — she walks out; they put the
    Dormouse in the teapot. Door in a tree → the long hall again: "Now I'll manage better this
    time" — key first, then mushroom, then into the garden. *Theme: learns from her mistakes.*
15. **Ch 8, the roses and the Queen.** Cards Two, Five, Seven painting white roses red (planted
    the wrong tree; heads at stake). Procession; Knave with the crown. Alice: "they're only a pack
    of cards"; answers the Queen back; "Off with her head!" — "Nonsense!"; King: "she is only a
    child". Gardeners sentenced; Alice hides them in a flower-pot; soldiers say "Their heads are
    gone". Duchess under sentence for boxing the Queen's ears. Croquet: hedgehogs, flamingoes,
    soldiers as arches.
16. **Ch 8, croquet and the Cat's head.** Flamingo twists round, hedgehog crawls off; everyone
    plays at once; the Queen shouts for heads "about once in a minute". Cheshire Cat's head
    appears (the promise from 12 kept); "likely to win"; "A cat may look at a king". The Queen's
    "only one way of settling all difficulties". Executioner can't behead a head with no body;
    "It belongs to the Duchess" — fetched from prison; the Cat fades. *Theme: the Queen settles
    everything the same way.*
17. **Ch 9, the Duchess's morals.** Duchess now friendly; Alice's rule that pepper makes people
    hot-tempered. "Every thing's got a moral." Sharp chin. The Queen: "either you or your head
    must be off" — the Duchess vanishes. All players but King, Queen and Alice in custody. "Have
    you seen the Mock Turtle yet?" The King quietly: "You are all pardoned."
18. **Ch 9, Gryphon and Mock Turtle.** Gryphon: "they never executes nobody"; the Mock Turtle's
    sorrow is "all his fancy". "Once I was a real Turtle." School in the sea; Tortoise; extras;
    lesson-puns; Alice made to feel dull ("ready to sink into the earth"); lessons ten hours,
    nine, and so on. *Theme: the Queen's orders come to nothing.*
19. **Ch 10, the Lobster Quadrille.** The dance explained; they dance it round Alice; the whiting
    song. Alice almost says she's tasted lobster / seen whiting at dinner. Whiting have their tails
    in their mouths; boots and soles; "porpoise".
20. **Ch 10, Alice's story and the Soup.** Alice tells her adventures; the Mock Turtle wants her
    to repeat something; "'Tis the voice of the Lobster" comes out wrong (her head full of the
    Quadrille); she sits with her face in her hands. "Beautiful Soup". "The trial's beginning!"
    *Theme: even things she knows go wrong in Wonderland (poems in 3, 9, 20).*
21. **Ch 11, the court.** King as judge (crown over wig); twelve jurors writing their names on
    slates so they won't forget; "stupid things!"; Alice takes Bill's squeaky pencil. The
    accusation: the Knave stole the tarts. "Consider your verdict" — "Not yet".
22. **Ch 11, the Hatter and the cook.** Hatter with teacup and bread-and-butter; three dates;
    hat not his own. Alice starts growing; the Dormouse complains. Queen asks for the list of
    singers from the last concert (link to 13) — the Hatter shakes off his shoes, bites his
    teacup. Guinea-pigs "suppressed" in a bag. Hatter escapes before "take his head off
    outside". Cook: "Pepper, mostly"; Dormouse: "Treacle"; turned out. Next witness: "Alice!"
    *Theme: why the Hatter fears the Queen.*
23. **Ch 12, Alice's evidence.** Tips the jury-box (goldfish memory); Bill put in upside down.
    "Nothing whatever"; important/unimportant. Rule Forty-two, "more than a mile high"; "Then it
    ought to be Number One." The unsigned verses — the King says not signing proves mischief;
    Alice: "you don't even know what they're about"; "not an atom of meaning in it". Now big
    enough not to be afraid of interrupting.
24. **Ch 12, the verdict and waking.** The King reads guilt into the verses; tarts on the table;
    inkstand at Bill; the pun. "Sentence first—verdict afterwards." "Off with her head!" — nobody
    moves. "You're nothing but a pack of cards!" The cards fly; she wakes on the bank in her
    sister's lap; "curious dream"; runs in to tea. The sister dreams it over, and pictures Alice
    grown up telling children the tale. *Theme: she has grown braver (vs. 7, running off for the
    Rabbit).*

## Threads

- Size changes: bottle/cake (2), fan (3), Rabbit's bottle and pebble-cakes (7–8), mushroom (9–10,
  14), growing in court (22–24). Size and courage rise together at the end.
- Who am I? (3, 9, 10). Poems that come out wrong (3, 9, 20).
- Dinah upsets the creatures (4, 6, 8).
- The garden: seen (2), reached (14).
- "Off with his head!": the concert (13), gardeners (15), croquet (16–17), pardon and "never
  executes nobody" (17–18), the Hatter's fear (22), Alice (24).
