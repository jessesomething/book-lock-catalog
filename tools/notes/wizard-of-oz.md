# The Wonderful Wizard of Oz — segment notes

What each segment covers and which threads are open, so a later segment's theme question can
reach back without rereading the book, and never forward. Theme evidence must quote this or an
earlier segment; the build rejects anything later.

**Segment starts reviewed through:** chapter 15 (segments 1–27). Chapters 16–23 are one segment
each (chapter 24 is folded into 23), so no further cuts to check.

## Segments

1. **Ch 1.** Gray Kansas: gray prairie, gray house, Aunt Em and Uncle Henry worn gray and
   joyless. Only Toto keeps Dorothy from "growing as gray". Cyclone; Toto hides, so Dorothy
   misses the cellar; the house flies; she sleeps.
2. **Ch 2, first half.** Lands in the colorful Munchkin country. The Witch of the North and three
   Munchkins thank her as a "noble Sorceress" for killing the Wicked Witch of the East — her house
   fell on her by accident. Silver shoes on the feet. Four witches: North and South good, East and
   West wicked. Oz is the Great Wizard in the City of Emeralds.
3. **Ch 2, second half.** The Witch's feet dry up; the silver shoes have "some charm" nobody
   knows. A desert surrounds Oz. The slate says go to the City of Emeralds; the Witch's kiss
   leaves a shining protective mark. Yellow brick road. "Whether he is a man or not I cannot tell."
4. **Ch 3, first part.** Dorothy puts on the silver shoes (hers are worn out) and sets out. Blue
   Munchkin country. Boq's party celebrating freedom; Boq also takes her for a sorceress (silver
   shoes, white in her frock); she knows she's "only an ordinary little girl". Boq warns of
   dangerous places ahead; she resolves not to turn back.
5. **Ch 3, the Scarecrow.** Lifts him off his pole. He wants brains so people won't call him a
   fool; joins her. Afraid only of a lighted match.
6. **Ch 4.** Scarecrow's mouth is painted, so he can't eat. "There is no place like home" — even
   gray Kansas. His origin story; the old crow: "Brains are the only things worth having".
   Forest; the Scarecrow reasons his way through things while insisting he has no brains; cottage
   for the night.
7. **Ch 5, first part.** Finds the rusted Tin Woodman; oils him with his own oil-can. He wants a
   heart and joins. He chops a path through.
8. **Ch 5, the Woodman's story.** The Wicked Witch of the East enchanted his axe (paid by an old
   woman to stop his marriage); a tinsmith replaced each lost part; with a tin body he lost his
   heart and his love for the Munchkin girl; rusted in a rainstorm. Brains vs heart: "happiness is
   the best thing in the world". Bread is running low.
9. **Ch 6.** The Lion attacks; Dorothy slaps his nose to save Toto and calls him a coward. He
   roars so others run, but is scared inside; "whenever there is danger, my heart begins to beat
   fast". Wants courage; joins. The Woodman steps on a beetle, cries, his jaws rust; the Scarecrow
   oils them. Having no heart, the Woodman takes "great care never to be cruel or unkind".
10. **Ch 7, first part.** Camp; last of the bread; the Scarecrow gathers nuts, keeps from the
    fire. First ditch: the Scarecrow reasons it out and goes first (a fall can't hurt him); the
    Lion, "terribly afraid of falling", jumps them all across.
11. **Ch 7, second part.** Kalidahs (bear bodies, tiger heads). The Scarecrow's tree-bridge idea
    — Lion: "One would almost suspect you had brains". The Lion, trembling, faces them; the
    Scarecrow has the bridge chopped and the Kalidahs fall. "I wish I had a heart to beat." River;
    raft started.
12. **Ch 8, first part.** Raft swept downstream toward the Wicked Witch of the West's country
    ("she will enchant us and make us her slaves"). The Scarecrow is left on his pole in the
    river ("worse off than when I first met Dorothy"). The Lion tows the raft ashore. A Stork, who
    likes "to help anyone in trouble", carries the Scarecrow back; he vows to repay her.
13. **Ch 8, the poppy field.** Dazzling scarlet poppies whose scent puts people to sleep forever.
    Dorothy and Toto fall asleep; the Lion runs ahead but falls asleep near the edge. The
    Scarecrow and Woodman (not flesh) carry Dorothy out; the Lion is left.
14. **Ch 9.** The Woodman kills a Wildcat to save the Queen of the Field Mice ("only a mouse!").
    The Scarecrow's plan: thousands of mice with strings pull the Lion out on a truck the Woodman
    builds. The Queen: "If ever you need us again … call" (open promise).
15. **Ch 10, first part.** The Lion wakes: "such little things as flowers came near to killing me,
    and such small animals as mice have saved my life". Back on the road; green country, unfriendly
    people. Farmhouse: no one has seen Oz; he can take any form; "Oz keeps a great pot of courage";
    Oz can do anything.
16. **Ch 10, the gate.** Green glow; the emerald gate; the Guardian of the Gates: Oz is "powerful
    and terrible", few dare ask to see his face. Green spectacles locked on everyone, for the
    city's "brightness and glory"; only the Guardian has the key.
17. **Ch 11, the city.** Green marble and emeralds, green people, green lemonade and pennies. The
    soldier has never seen Oz ("behind his screen"); one visitor a day, alone. Rooms: the
    Scarecrow stands all night watching a spider; the Woodman lies down from habit; the Lion
    curls up like a cat.
18. **Ch 11, Dorothy's audience.** Courtiers who never see Oz. Oz agreed after hearing of the
    silver shoes and the kiss mark. A giant Head: "I am Oz, the Great and Terrible." "Dorothy, the
    Small and Meek." Killing the East Witch "just happened". "Everyone must pay for everything he
    gets": kill the Wicked Witch of the West first. "The silver shoes, which bear a powerful
    charm." She cries herself to sleep.
19. **Ch 11, the others.** Oz as a winged Lady to the Scarecrow ("she needs a heart as much as the
    Tin Woodman"), a five-eyed Beast to the Woodman (not afraid, being tin), a Ball of Fire to the
    Lion. All must help kill the Witch. Each says he'll be little use, but all go. The green girl
    fills the basket; a bell on Toto.
20. **Ch 12, first part.** No road west. Dorothy's dress turns from green to white outside the
    city. The Witch's one telescope eye; silver whistle. Wolves (Woodman's axe), crows (the
    Scarecrow — crows fear scarecrows), bees (straw covers the others; stings break on tin).
21. **Ch 12, second part.** Winkie slaves flee the Lion's roar. The Golden Cap: three calls of the
    Winged Monkeys; the Witch has used two (enslaving the Winkies, driving Oz out of the West) and
    uses the last. Woodman dropped on rocks, Scarecrow unstuffed and his clothes thrown in a tree,
    Lion caged. Kiss mark: "the Power of Good … is greater than the Power of Evil." The Witch fears
    the shoes but sees Dorothy doesn't know their power; makes her a kitchen slave.
22. **Ch 12, third part.** The Lion refuses the harness; Dorothy feeds him nightly. The Witch can't
    strike Dorothy (mark); Toto bites her — no blood. The Witch fears dark and water. Invisible iron
    bar trick takes one shoe; Dorothy throws water; the Witch melts ("Didn't you know water would be
    the end of me?" "Of course not"). Dorothy puts the shoe back on; runs to free the Lion.
23. **Ch 13.** Dorothy frees the Winkies, who keep the day as a holiday ever after. Winkie
    tinsmiths mend the Woodman (patches; "not a vain man"); tears of joy wiped away. Tree chopped,
    the Scarecrow restuffed. Gold axe-handle. Winkies ask the Woodman to rule them; parting gifts.
    Dorothy takes the Golden Cap because it is pretty, not knowing its charm.
24. **Ch 14, first part.** Lost in the fields (can't tell east from west at noon). Everyone loses
    heart. Dorothy calls the field mice (text says with a whistle the Queen gave her — contradicts
    ch 9; don't ask about it). The Queen explains the Cap's charm. Dorothy summons the Monkeys
    (first of her three calls); they carry everyone.
25. **Ch 14, the Monkey King's story.** Once free and mischievous. Gayelette and Quelala; the prank
    in the river; spared on condition of obeying the Cap's owner three times. Quelala's use; then
    the Wicked Witch's (Winkies, driving Oz from the West). Now Dorothy's. Set down at the gate.
26. **Ch 15, first part.** Back in the city; days of waiting; the Scarecrow threatens the Monkeys
    and Oz (who met them before) gives in. Empty Throne Room, a Voice. The Lion roars, Toto knocks
    over the screen: a little old bald man. "I have been making believe." "I am a humbug." No one
    else knows. Kept hidden so people believe he is terrible.
27. **Ch 15, second part.** Paper Head on a wire, ventriloquism, the Lady's mask, the Beast of
    skins, the Ball of Fire of oiled cotton. Born in Omaha; circus balloonist; blown to Oz and taken
    for a wizard. Built the city; green spectacles make it green. Feared the Witches. "A very good
    man, but a very bad Wizard." To the Scarecrow: "Experience is the only thing that brings
    knowledge." To the Lion: "True courage is in facing danger when you are afraid." To the
    Woodman: a heart "makes most people unhappy". Promises gifts tomorrow; needs days for Kansas.
    Keep his secret.
28. **Ch 16.** Dorothy: "I have always liked you as you were." Bran-and-pins brains, a silk heart
    stuffed with sawdust, courage from a green bottle ("courage is always inside one"). Oz: easy to
    make them happy "because they imagined I could do anything"; Kansas will take more than
    imagination.
29. **Ch 17.** The friends content; the Woodman's heart "kinder and more tender". Oz plans a
    hot-air balloon and will go too — tired of being a humbug shut up in his rooms. The Scarecrow
    left to rule. Toto chases a kitten; the ropes break; Oz floats away without Dorothy. The
    people remember him lovingly.
30. **Ch 18.** The Woodman mourns Oz. The Scarecrow rules, "satisfied with my lot". He thinks hard
    (pins stick out): the Monkeys — second call, wasted; they can't leave Oz. The soldier: Glinda,
    Witch of the South, on the desert's edge. All three go; the Scarecrow owes his luck to Dorothy.
31. **Ch 19.** Farewells at the gate. "Oz was not such a bad Wizard, after all"; Dorothy forgives
    him — "a good man, even if he was a bad Wizard". Fighting trees; the Woodman chops through;
    Toto shaken. A white china wall.
32. **Ch 20.** Over the wall into the china country: tiny brittle people. The cow's broken leg,
    the angry milkmaid. The Princess won't be chased (mended is never as pretty); Mr. Joker, cracked
    all over. She won't go to Kansas — away from home she'd stiffen; "our lives are much pleasanter
    here in our own country". The Lion's tail smashes a church. Scarecrow: "There are worse things
    in the world than being a Scarecrow."
33. **Ch 21.** Bogs; a delightful old forest (the Lion). The animals hail the King of Beasts; a
    giant spider has eaten the other lions. He kills it while it sleeps; they bow; he promises to
    return once Dorothy is on her way.
34. **Ch 22.** Armless Hammer-Heads with shooting heads knock back the Scarecrow and the Lion. Third
    and last call of the Monkeys carries them over. Red Quadling country. Glinda's castle.
35. **Ch 23–24.** Glinda on a ruby throne. Dorothy gives her the Golden Cap; Glinda will use its
    three calls to send each friend to his kingdom (Scarecrow — Emerald City; Woodman — Winkies;
    Lion — his forest) and then free the Monkeys forever. The silver shoes could have taken her home
    the first day; the friends are glad she didn't know. Three heel clicks: "Take me home to Aunt
    Em!" The shoes fall off in the desert. Aunt Em; "I'm so glad to be at home again!"

**All 35 segments written and built (2026-09-25).**

## Open threads

- **The silver shoes' unknown charm** (seg 3) — pays off at the end. Keep hinting, never reveal.
- **The kiss mark** (seg 2–3) protects Dorothy from harm.
- **Gray vs color** (seg 1–2).
- **Each companion lacks something and hopes Oz will supply it**: home (Dorothy), brains
  (Scarecrow), heart (Tin Woodman), courage (Lion, seg 9). The Scarecrow already thinks well, the
  Woodman already feels deeply, the Lion already acts bravely while afraid — the book's central
  irony. **Stated outright from seg 27** (Oz: experience brings knowledge; "True courage is in
  facing danger when you are afraid"); questions may now say they had it all along.
- **Oz is a humbug** (revealed seg 26–27): hid so people would fear him; the forms were tricks;
  "a very good man, but a very bad Wizard". Must keep his secret.
- **Green spectacles** make the city green (revealed seg 27; hinted by the white dress, seg 20).
- **Golden Cap**: three calls per owner. Dorothy owns it now (seg 23) and used her **first** call
  to fly to the city (seg 24). Two left.
- **The Monkeys drove Oz out of the West** (seg 21).
- **Silver shoes**: "a powerful charm" (seg 18), the Witch fears them, Dorothy doesn't know how to
  use them (seg 21). Still unrevealed.
- **Dorothy defeats both Wicked Witches by accident** (seg 2, 22), and "never killed anything,
  willingly" (seg 18).
- **The Scarecrow and Woodman are "destroyed"** (seg 21) — Woodman dented on rocks, Scarecrow's
  clothes in a tree.
- **Dorothy mistaken for a sorceress** because of chance, not power (seg 2, 4).
- **Queen of the Field Mice's promise** to come if called (seg 14).
- **Kindness repaid**: the Stork (seg 12), the mice (seg 14).
- **Wicked Witch of the West** enslaves travelers who stray into her country (seg 3, 12).
- **The Lion's "heart beats fast" in danger** while the Woodman wishes for one (seg 9, 11).
