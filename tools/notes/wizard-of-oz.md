# The Wonderful Wizard of Oz — segment notes

What each segment covers and which threads are open, so a later segment's theme question can
reach back without rereading the book, and never forward. Theme evidence must quote this or an
earlier segment; the build rejects anything later.

**Segment starts reviewed through:** chapter 5 (segments 1–8). Starts from chapter 7 on are
still the script's proposal; check each against a real scene break before writing its quiz.

## Segments

1. **Ch 1.** Gray Kansas: gray prairie, gray house, Aunt Em and Uncle Henry worn gray and
   joyless. Only Toto keeps Dorothy from "growing as gray". Cyclone; Toto hides, so Dorothy
   misses the cellar; the house flies; she sleeps.
2. **Ch 2, first half.** Lands in the colorful Munchkin country. The Witch of the North and three
   Munchkins thank her as a "noble Sorceress" for killing the Wicked Witch of the East — her house
   fell on her by accident. Silver shoes on the feet. Four witches: North and South good, East and
   West wicked. Oz is the Great Wizard in the City of Emeralds.
3. **Ch 2, second half.** The Witch's feet dry up; the silver shoes have "some charm" nobody
   knows. A desert surrounds Oz. The slate says go to the City of Emeralds; the Witch's kiss
   leaves a shining protective mark. Yellow brick road. "Whether he is a man or not I cannot tell."
4. **Ch 3, first part.** Dorothy puts on the silver shoes (hers are worn out) and sets out. Blue
   Munchkin country. Boq's party celebrating freedom; Boq also takes her for a sorceress (silver
   shoes, white in her frock); she knows she's "only an ordinary little girl". Boq warns of
   dangerous places ahead; she resolves not to turn back.
5. **Ch 3, the Scarecrow.** Lifts him off his pole. He wants brains so people won't call him a
   fool; joins her. Afraid only of a lighted match.
6. **Ch 4.** Scarecrow's mouth is painted, so he can't eat. "There is no place like home" — even
   gray Kansas. His origin story; the old crow: "Brains are the only things worth having".
   Forest; the Scarecrow reasons his way through things while insisting he has no brains; cottage
   for the night.
7. **Ch 5, first part.** Finds the rusted Tin Woodman; oils him with his own oil-can. He wants a
   heart and joins. He chops a path through.
8. **Ch 5, the Woodman's story.** The Wicked Witch of the East enchanted his axe (paid by an old
   woman to stop his marriage); a tinsmith replaced each lost part; with a tin body he lost his
   heart and his love for the Munchkin girl; rusted in a rainstorm. Brains vs heart: "happiness is
   the best thing in the world". Bread is running low.

## Open threads

- **The silver shoes' unknown charm** (seg 3) — pays off at the end. Keep hinting, never reveal.
- **The kiss mark** (seg 2–3) protects Dorothy from harm.
- **Gray vs color** (seg 1–2).
- **Each companion lacks something and hopes Oz will supply it**: home (Dorothy), brains
  (Scarecrow), heart (Tin Woodman). The Scarecrow already thinks well and the Woodman already
  feels deeply — the book's central irony. Don't state it outright before the book does; a
  question that depends on that reading is debatable until then.
- **Nobody has seen Oz** (seg 3) — sets up the reveal.
- **Dorothy mistaken for a sorceress** because of chance, not power (seg 2, 4).
