# The Railway Children — segment notes

What each segment covers and which threads are open, so a later segment's theme question can
reach back without rereading the book, and never forward. Theme evidence must quote this or an
earlier segment; the build rejects anything later.

**Segment starts reviewed:** all 37 (every mid-chapter start checked against the text and moved
onto a time/place shift or episode end where the proposal cut mid-scene). Chapters 1, 5, 8, 12
and 14 are two segments each; the rest are three. Sizes run 6.1–14.6 minutes. Unavoidable
mid-scene cuts (no clean break inside a long continuous scene): seg 13 (Mother's train arrives
at the station), seg 16 (the wait for the train, after the flags are ready), seg 24 (the
children hide as Perks comes home).

**Chapter questions** have 4 choices; segment quizzes reuse them verbatim (prompt, choice text,
evidence) but keep 3 choices, dropping a wrong one — first any that names something from later
in the book (Aunt Emma arriving, the old gentleman coming to tea, the doctor's bill, etc.).

**Text oddities (don't ask about them):** Mother's verses, Peter's engine-number list, the
banners' wording ("LOOK OUT AT THE STATION"), the brake-van label and Mother's lesson rhymes
were stripped from the source, so some paragraphs end in a colon with nothing after.

## Segments

1. **Ch 1, to "we WON'T know it".** Ordinary suburban children (Roberta, Peter, Phyllis) in the
   Red Villa; perfect Father. Peter's birthday engine goes off with a bang; Father promises to
   mend it Saturday — "Girls are just as clever as boys"; Roberta wants to be an engine-driver.
   Knock: two gentlemen, Father's voice "louder and different". Ruth: "bad news". Mother white:
   "Father's been called away—on business." Roberta hears Mother crying: "we WON'T know it."
2. **Ch 1, rest.** Mother home late, ill: Father away "for some time"; be good, don't quarrel,
   **don't ask questions**. Father "in a Government Office". "It'll all come right in the end."
   Horrid weeks; Aunt Emma (going abroad as a governess). Ruth: "you'll go where your precious
   Father's gone" — Ruth sent away. Mother ill; moving to "a ducky dear little white house";
   packing the ugly useful things — "play at being Poor for a bit". Peter: "I do like moving!"
   Mother laughs; Roberta sees her face: "how brave you are!" Dark arrival, walk, no Mrs. Viney,
   key under the doorstep, bare kitchen, rats. "ONLY the rats!"
3. **Ch 2, first night and morning.** Mother: "What fun!" No supper; packing-case opened with
   coal-shovel and poker; fourteen candles; "The great thing is to be cheerful." Aunt Emma's odds
   and ends make a feast — they toast her, feel they'd been hard on her. Next morning the
   children rise early to be useful, wash at the pump, light fire, see the railway and tunnel,
   fall asleep on a stone; kettle boiled dry. The forgotten room (mistaken for a cupboard) holds
   Mrs. Viney's supper; apple pie for breakfast.
4. **Ch 2, the railway.** Garden found behind the stables. First train out of the tunnel "like
   a great dragon". Roberta is "Bobbie" from now on. "London's where Father is." Station joys;
   the Porter: whitewash line on the coal "so as we'll know if anyone nicks it" (words come back
   to Peter later). Dough image: sorrow made "a deep impression" that didn't last; used to being
   without Father "though they did not forget him". Mother writing all day, reading stories at
   tea. "Quite poor now" didn't seem real.
5. **Ch 2, the coal-mine.** Three cold wet June days; no fires, "coal is so dear". Jam OR butter.
   Peter's secret idea "MAY be wrong"; "say I'm playing at mines". The Roman Chariot (old pram).
   Caught on the station coal heap by the Station Master: "I'm not a thief... I'm a
   coal-miner." Bobbie: "It's our fault just as much as Peter's." Peter: "You know that wet day?"
   Let off: "stealing is stealing". Peter: "spies and traitors"; "not at all sure... mining is a
   crime" — but the girls were sure he was sure.
6. **Ch 3, the old gentleman.** Trains named: 9.15 up the Green Dragon, 10.7 down the Worm of
   Wantley, midnight express the Fearsome Fly-by-night. They wave to send love to Father; the
   old gentleman in a first-class carriage waves back, daily. Aunt Emma's gaiters useful after
   all. Mother's stories rejected ("come home to roost") or taken — buns for tea. Peter feels
   "'Stolen'... was the right one", runs after the Station Master to say sorry: "Let bygones be
   bygones." "She used to play with us more before she had to be so clever."
7. **Ch 3, at the station.** Peter and Phyllis squabble (bootlace, "ungentlemanly"); Bobbie makes
   peace ("let honour be satisfied"). Two hours with the Porter: couplings, brake pipes, the
   communication cord and the old lady's bath bun; trains are "she". Oranges from the Station
   Master. Peter starts collecting engine numbers; Mother gives him a black notebook. Walking on
   the line: Mother did it too as a child; "No one... ever loved anyone more than my mother loved
   me"; Bobbie kicks Phyllis, understanding Mother has no mother to run to. Mother allows it —
   not near the tunnel or corners.
8. **Ch 3, Mother's illness.** Influenza; Dr. Forrest ("Lady Grave-airs", head-nurse). Mother
   "can't afford all that rubbish". Peter and Phyllis paint a sheet; Bobbie nurses all night;
   Mother calls for Granny. The sheet by the line for the 9.15; Phyllis gives the old gentleman
   a letter ("Peter will pay you when he is a man... I.O.U."). The Porter brings the hamper
   and sweetbrier, refuses twopence. Hamper: everything and more; letter says tell Mother later,
   "you were quite right"; signed G. P. Peter dreads telling Mother.
9. **Ch 4, to the aqueduct.** A thank-you banner for the 9.15. They tell Mother: extremely
   angry, then cries (a "crying-party"); we're poor but "you mustn't go telling everyone about our
   affairs"; "never, never, never ask strangers to give you things". Mother writes to thank the
   old gentleman. "Isn't Mother splendid?" (said sorry for being angry). The Porter is **Perks**,
   married with three children; head-lights/tail-lights; railwaymen call the 9.15 "the Snail".
   Bobbie's birthday: sent out of the way; worries about "what a doctor's bill there'll be";
   canal bridge — railway people kind, **canal people (bargees) unkind** (coal thrown at Phyllis).
   Doctor's dogcart: "you aren't everyone"; asks to join the Club like Mrs. Viney; the Doctor,
   "rather poor himself", agrees to make it right. The Aqueduct, "a water bridge".
10. **Ch 4, the birthday party.** Bobbie feels "for once she was the one who was right". Twelve
    candles (she is 12), forget-me-not wreath, Mother's song. Presents: needle-book (Phyllis),
    Mother's **buttercup brooch**, blue glass vases (Mrs. Viney). Flower map of the railway —
    daisies are the three waving to the old gentleman (pansy). Peter's engine: only the sweets —
    then "go halves". Bobbie resolves to get the broken half mended and give it back for his
    birthday. Games; Mother "would only just write to Father"; Bobbie finds her with her head on
    her arms: "She doesn't want me to know she's unhappy, and I won't know".
11. **Ch 4, the engine-burglar.** Mother (and Peter, Phyllis) go to town; she posts her letters
    to Father herself. Bobbie's frock tears on a nail. She climbs into the engine cab to ask the
    men; the train moves; "I'm an engine-burglar". Bill (driver) and Jim (fireman): "Daring little
    piece", "Mate". "Everybody that has anything to do with railways is so kind and good." Offers
    to pay back a ticket: "a little lady, every inch". Injector, automatic brake. Jim's second
    cousin's wife's brother will solder it. Home via Stacklepoole Junction and a fishy guard's
    van. Later: engine "as good as new", given to Peter.
12. **Ch 5, the stranger.** Wet July day waiting for Mother's train; advertisement game. Bill and
    Jim pass (toffee; may give Peter a ride one day). Crowd round a wild-eyed shabby man speaking
    an unknown language; Peter's "Parlay voo Frongsay?" gets French back. Bobbie: "He thinks you're
    going to shut him up"; her French "Nous etre bong pour vous"; he understood the touch of her
    hand. Station Master's room; he cries; Bobbie's handkerchief. Stamps: a Russian stamp. Bobbie
    holds his hand till Mother comes.
13. **Ch 5, the Russian's story.** Mother talks French; he's lost his ticket, is ill, "a great man
    in his own country, writes books". Bobbie fetches the Doctor ("I never saw a man cry before").
    The locked trunk: **Father's clothes** left behind; "Daddy isn't DEAD, is he?" — "My darling,
    no!... he'll come back to us some day." Bedtime: a book about poor people → three years in a
    dungeon, Siberia in chains, the mines for life; volunteered, deserted to find his **wife and
    children**, who escaped to England. "people only go to prison when they've done wrong" — "Or
    when the Judges THINK they've done wrong". Mother's voice unsteady: pray for "all prisoners
    and captives".
14. **Ch 6, Perks offended.** Russian recovering in Father's hemmed clothes; Mother writes to MPs,
    editors. Phyllis's idea: Perks's strawberries. Perks cold — nobody told him the Russian's
    story. Peter: not fair to punish without saying why. Phyllis hugs him; they tell the story on
    the bank. Perks on taking sides ("Party Politics"). Phyllis honestly admits she came for the
    strawberries. Perks springs across the line in front of a train.
15. **Ch 6, the landslide.** Wild cherries as a surprise for the Russian; lunch and Mother's watch.
    At the cutting the trees "walk" ("like the woods in Macbeth"); rock and trees fall on the line.
    The 11.29 down is coming; station two miles off, no time. Something red; "they'd only think it
    was just US" waving. Phyllis's red flannel petticoats; torn into six flags on sapling sticks.
    Peter takes two ("my idea"); Bobbie: "what does it matter who waves what, if we can only save
    the train?"
16. **Ch 6, saving the train.** Long wait; Bobbie waves her two flags over the line ("Not yet!");
    the train stops twenty yards off. Bobbie faints across the line, gripping the flags; carried to
    a first-class carriage; wakes and cries. Heroes at the station — Phyllis loves it, Peter too;
    Bobbie wants to go home. Cheers. "How lucky we DID put on our red flannel petticoats!" Bobbie,
    thinking of the mound: "We never got any cherries, after all" — others think her heartless.
17. **Ch 7, before the presentation.** Narrator on Bobbie: anxious to make others happy, keeps
    secrets, "silent sympathy" — "Mother's unhappy. Why? I don't know... I won't try to find
    out." "Loving-kindness" = trying to help people. The Russian is **Mr. Szezcpansky**; learning
    English. Bobbie's nightmares of the mound. Letter: a presentation on the 30th. Mother: if it's
    money, "we'd rather not take it". Washing Indian muslins ("you don't rub muslin"). Guesses
    (baby elephant, gold engine models, medals). Bobbie: "do you LIKE us being rewarded?... oughtn't
    we to be satisfied with just having done it". Peter: V.C. soldiers are glad to get it.
    Marriage chatter.
18. **Ch 7, the presentation.** Pink-paper reply: "Thank you very much" over and over, P.S. too.
    Decorated waiting room; the old gentleman there; District Superintendent's long speech. Old
    gentleman gives each a **gold watch and chain** ("From the Directors of the Northern and
    Southern Railway"). Peter's speech ("we don't deserve it"). Bobbie wanted to talk to the old
    gentleman privately; writes: "Only to talk to you about a Prisoner and Captive."
19. **Ch 7, the Russian's family.** Phyllis's lemonade-and-coal-dust frock; shyness. Bobbie tells
    the story; would give up the watches. "Fryingpansky" — Szezcpansky, said Shepansky. "THAT man?
    ... I've read his book!... your mother must be a very good woman." He knows many Russians in
    London. Phyllis brings tea in Perks's can. Peter: "we've lighted a candle". Ten days later the
    old gentleman crosses the field: found his wife and child. Bobbie runs to tell; the Russian's
    cry; she creeps away. Chocolates. The Russian leaves. Mother can't ask the old gentleman to
    come again ("we live very quietly"). Peter "head man of the family when Father's away".
    Phyllis: will Father think I've grown? — Mother's hands tighten; Bobbie starts a race she
    hated, to stop the talk; Mother thought she was tired of walking slowly.
20. **Ch 8, to the bargewoman.** Perks admires the buttercup brooch; he's 32 on the fifteenth,
    doesn't keep birthdays — "the kids and the Missus". Peter's fishing line from the postman
    ("the grateful heart"; Bobbie wrongly assumed a swap and apologises). Canal bridge at sunset;
    fishing from the towpath; the Bargee ("get out of that") grabs Peter by the ear — "these 'ere
    waters is preserved"; Peter truthfully "I WASN'T catching fish"; Bobbie rushes down. He goes to
    the 'Rose and Crown'. Peter won't be driven off the bridge. His wife: "'is bark's worse'n 'is
    bite"; baby asleep in the cabin with Spot the dog.
21. **Ch 8, the fire.** Dusk; smoke from the cabin door; Peter "Serve him right" — "The BABY!"
    Peter falls in the canal, then goes in because he's wet; pushes Bobbie back twice; wet
    handkerchief; "hardly any fire at all" (to keep her out); finds the baby. Spot bites Bobbie
    gently. Bobbie runs to the 'Rose and Crown': "Your barge cabin's on fire"; "Reginald Horace!"
    Fire out; Bill's pipe ash caused it and he didn't blame his wife. Mother: they did right. Free
    trip to Farley and back, nineteen locks, over the aqueduct; Mother and Father used to boat at
    Marlow. Bargees to treat them as pals (red ribbon). "First the railway and then the canal!"
    Bobbie: "everyone in the world is friends if you can only get them to see you don't want to be
    UN-friends." Perks's birthday not yet planned.
22. **Ch 9, planning Perks's birthday.** Mother sells "the King of the Mussels"; buns saved for
    the fifteenth; A. P. (Albert Perks) in pink sugar. Hay-loft council; Peter: ask everyone in
    the village — "For ourselves, she meant, silly, not for other people"; won't ask Mother first.
    Post-office old lady grumpy — her birthday is tomorrow, "much notice anyone will take of it".
    Bobbie and Phyllis secretly leave her roses, the needle-book and a necktie ("For Mrs. Ransome").
    Mother: fine, but "I only hope he won't be offended and think it's CHARITY. Poor people are
    very proud." Phyllis: "it's because we're fond of him." Mother gives outgrown clothes; Bobbie
    scribbles her words down.
23. **Ch 9, to the Perkses' house.** Pink-sugar A. P. buns. Mrs. Ransome returns the basket full
    of gooseberries and gives **her pram** (her Emmie's baby's, who died at six months) for
    Mrs. Perks. The Perkses' little yellow house; Mrs. Perks had cleaned up because Perks mentioned
    his birthday; bursts into tears — "a birthday such as Perks never 'ad". Tea laid "fit for a
    Prince". Perks arrives early; Bobbie's plan to hide and shout "Many happy returns!"
24. **Ch 9, the labels.** Crammed in the wash-house; Bobbie forgot the labels. Perks: "charity
    goings-on", "not if it was a angel from Heaven"; "It ain't what you means so much as what you
    does." "You've disgraced us"; turns his chair away. Phyllis: "you were wrong once before".
    Bobbie reads the labels (Mother's "born lady"; Mrs. Ransome's pram; James the smith's shovel;
    the old gentleman's gold pound; everyone likes and respects him). Perks takes it all back —
    "the kind respect of our neighbours". "May the garland of friendship be ever green." "It's not
    so much what you does as what you means." Mrs. Perks: "just friendliness"; the clergyman:
    "loving-kindness".
25. **Ch 10, Bobbie and Mother.** The children speak less of Father; Bobbie stopped because it
    made Mother worse. Mother: besides the parting, "a great sorrow"; forgetting him "would be
    worse than anything"; the worst will be over "when Father comes home". Mother has noticed
    them not quarrelling and doing kind things. Evening stories of Father and Mother's brothers
    as children; Uncle Edward (died, "only away for a little time"), Uncle Reggie in Ceylon.
    Bobbie: not dead, not ill, not money — "something nearer the heart"; "We'll keep that up"
    (not quarrelling).
26. **Ch 10, the rake.** The three gardens (Phyllis's flowers; Peter's forts and canals; Bobbie's
    dead roses — Perks offers plants). The rake quarrel ("I had it first"; "whiny little kiddy
    sisters"; "why little boys were ever invented"). Bobbie sees Mother's windows, remembers the
    praise, can't say "Don't let's quarrel", lets go; Peter falls, three rake wounds, faints.
    Bobbie: "he's not a coward!" Phyllis for the Doctor. Bobbie: "I wish I'd never been born";
    Perks comforts her, plants her garden. Doctor: off the foot a week, fine in a fortnight.
27. **Ch 10, the terrible secret.** Bobbie: "ALL my fault"; Peter: "don't drivel", don't be a
    "Sunday-school prig", "I'm glad it was ME". "Pax." Peter on the settle for days; "he's not a
    coward" keeps him from fussing — "Praise helps people very much, sometimes." Visitors;
    nothing to read. Perks: a guinea-pig coming, illustrated papers wrapped in an old newspaper.
    At the level-crossing Bobbie reads it; locks herself in: "End of the Trial. Verdict.
    Sentence." Father's name; "Guilty"; "Five years' Penal Servitude." "You never did it!
    Never, never, never!" Called down to tea.
28. **Ch 11, Bobbie tells Mother.** Tearful tea ("NOT before the others!"). Alone: shows the paper.
    "You don't BELIEVE it?" "NO." Mother: not true; "good and noble and honourable... be proud of
    him, and wait." Bobbie won't tell the others either — "Exactly". The whole story: the two men
    arrested him for **selling State secrets to the Russians** — "a spy and a traitor"; letters in
    his desk; "Someone put them there"; the man under him who got his place was jealous. "Nobody
    will listen." Be brave, patient, pray. Mother "very thin"; "bravest person in the world". "Try
    to be cheerful." Peter drills Phyllis not to ask.
29. **Ch 11, the letter and the paperchase.** A week later Bobbie writes to the old gentleman
    ("Think if it was YOUR Daddy"; "She told us once to pray for all prisoners and captives. I see
    now."), encloses the trial report, gives it to the Station Master. Peter and Phyllis make clay
    swallows' nests (F. for Phyllis; can swallows read?). Grammar School paperchase. At the
    cutting, workmen clearing the landslip. The hare, then thirty hounds, run into the tunnel —
    the last in a red jersey "extinguished... like a candle". They cut across the hill.
30. **Ch 11, into the tunnel.** Stiff climb ("Mount Everlasting"); the view from the top. Above
    the tunnel mouth: hare and hounds come out — not the red jersey. Peter fears an accident. Into
    the tunnel (slimy green bricks); Phyllis wants to go back; a train — the manhole; "It IS a
    dragon". On by candle: the red-jerseyed boy against the wall, fainted. Milk, rubbed hands,
    Phyllis's shuttlecock feathers burned. "Oh, look up, speak to me!"
31. **Ch 12, Bobbie and Jim.** He wakes ("Chuck it"); a wire tripped him; broken leg. Carried to
    the manhole. Bobbie stays ("Leave him here all alone because it's dark? Nonsense."). "SILLY
    little girl!" Cuts off boot and stocking; her white flannel petticoat as a cushion (remembers
    the red ones: "what useful things flannel petticoats are!"). He's **Jim**; "you're a brick";
    "just as brave as a boy"; "Somebody had to stay with you." Candle blown out to save the other;
    they hold hands in the dark. Cold.
32. **Ch 12, the signal-box.** Peter and Phyllis trudge out ("everything has an end, and you get
    to it if you only keep all on"). Peter: Phyllis went not from bravery but because they
    "aren't skunks". Signal-box: not allowed — Phyllis less afraid of doing wrong than of the
    tunnel. The **signalman is asleep**; Peter wakes him; he pulls the levers at 12.13, weeps with
    relief. His little boy has pneumonia; no sleep in five days. Peter: "we aren't sneaks". Offers
    two shillings to keep quiet — Phyllis: "You ARE a nasty man"; Peter knocks the money away.
    Phyllis forgives him ("want of sleep sends people mad"), "Kiss and be friends". Brigden's farm
    men with a hurdle; Bobbie and Jim asleep. Jim at school in Maidbridge, father in Northumberland,
    mother dead. Bobbie: bring him to Three Chimneys — "She took the poor Russian home herself."
    Mother ("a hound in a red jersey"... "I really CAN'T have a lame dog here") takes him in and
    kisses him like Peter.
33. **Ch 13, bone-setting.** Doctor sets Jim's leg upstairs. Peter, in a fit of "reaction", keeps
    on about blood and crunching bones; suggests playing at bone-setting; the girls tie him to the
    settle (from Stalky) until he promises to stop. The Doctor: "Playing at prisoners, eh?"; "a
    very heartless game". Peter owns up ("don't you go on being noble and screening me"). Untied;
    Peter goes to the surgery with the Doctor.
34. **Ch 13, the Doctor's talk; Peter and Mother.** Doctor's rescue stories; "scientific" talk:
    be careful of fists and words with girls and women — "awfully brave": Bobbie in the tunnel,
    "your Mother's one". Peter: "It's Pax" (garbles it as "female beasts"); Bobbie: "he's sorry,
    really... Let's say we're sorry"; "let honour be satisfied on both sides". Peter asks Mother
    not to write to Jim's grandfather — wants "another chap to talk to"; school next year perhaps;
    can't afford a nurse. "Wouldn't it be jolly if we all WERE in a book... and Father come home
    soon". "Do you miss your Father?" "Awfully." Mother: "we're in a book that God's writing";
    "Courage, courage! That's the finest of all the virtues!"
35. **Ch 13, the hound's grandfather.** Peter angelic, then back to normal. Cleaning brass
    candlesticks; a known voice upstairs; wild guesses. **Jim's grandfather is the old gentleman**
    ("just exactly like a book"). Jim stays; Mother to give up writing and be Matron of "Three
    Chimneys Hospital" (housemaid and cook); "perhaps something nice may happen and she won't have
    to"; "Very wonderful and beautiful things do happen". Jim called for "Mother" at night — "He
    didn't mean me". "A woman in a million." At the gate: got Bobbie's letter, "it wasn't needed";
    doubts from the start; "I have hopes... great hopes"; keep the secret a little longer; "I'm
    perfectly CERTAIN he didn't." Hope like a Japanese lantern.
36. **Ch 14, waiting.** Life changed: the old gentleman's name kept secret; servants Clara and
    Ethelwyn; Mrs. Viney two days a week; Mother gives lessons and rhymes; Jim's school tales
    (Parr, Wigsby Minor, Paley Terts), chess. "I wish something would happen" — it did, four days
    later. They'd stopped being railway children and waving to the 9.15; begin again. September.
    "Take our love to Father!" — every window waves: the old gentleman had told the passengers
    to read a part of their newspapers. Bobbie: "as if something was going to happen". Phyllis's
    stocking.
37. **Ch 14, the end.** Bobbie can't do sums; let off lessons; slate cracked. To the station:
    Post-office lady "God bless you, love", draper's boy polite, blacksmith "I wish you joy", Station
    Master pumps her hand, the Station Cat, Perks kisses her ("I see it in the paper"). The 11.54:
    chickens, Miss Peckitt, and — "Oh! my Daddy, my Daddy!" They've caught the man who did it;
    "it's all his doing" (the old gentleman). Father waits in the garden; the swallows are the
    ones the children built nests for. "Come in, Daddy; come in!" The narrator leaves them.
